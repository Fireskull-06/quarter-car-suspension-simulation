## 18. Response Plots

The time-domain responses of the passive and LQR-controlled suspension systems were plotted using the outputs obtained from the Simulink simulations.

The same road disturbance was applied to both configurations, allowing the dynamic responses to be compared directly.

### Body Acceleration

The body acceleration response is the primary indicator of ride comfort.

The passive and LQR responses are plotted together to show the reduction in vehicle-body vibration achieved by the active suspension.


The LQR controller significantly reduces the magnitude of the body acceleration response following the road disturbance.

### Suspension Travel

The suspension travel response represents the relative movement between the sprung and unsprung masses.


The LQR-controlled suspension produces a slightly lower RMS suspension travel compared with the passive suspension.

### Tire Deflection

The tire deflection response represents the relative displacement between the unsprung mass and the road surface.


The LQR controller results in a small increase in RMS tire deflection compared with the passive suspension.

### Plot Interpretation

The response plots provide a time-domain visualization of the controller's effect on the suspension dynamics.

The most significant change is observed in body acceleration, which corresponds to the primary ride-comfort objective. Suspension travel remains approximately
unchanged, while tire deflection experiences only a small increase.

Together with the RMS performance metrics, these plots demonstrate the overall effect of the LQR controller on the quarter-car suspension system.
