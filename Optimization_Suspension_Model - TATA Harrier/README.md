# 2-DOF Quarter-Car Suspension Model — Tata Harrier

## Overview

This project develops and simulates a **2-degree-of-freedom (2-DOF) quarter-car suspension model** of the **Tata Harrier** using MATLAB/Simulink.

The model represents the vertical dynamics of a quarter of the vehicle and consists of two masses: the **sprung mass**, representing the vehicle body, and the **unsprung mass**, representing the wheel and associated components. The suspension system is represented using a spring and damper, while the tire is represented by its vertical stiffness.

A road disturbance is applied as the input to the model to evaluate the response of the vehicle suspension. The simulation is used to study **ride comfort, suspension travel, and tire deflection**.

The 2-DOF model extends the earlier 1-DOF suspension model by explicitly modeling the interaction between the vehicle body and the wheel assembly.

## Objectives

The main objectives of the model are:

* Develop a realistic 2-DOF quarter-car representation of the **Tata Harrier**.
* Model the vertical interaction between the sprung and unsprung masses.
* Study the effect of a road disturbance on vehicle body motion.
* Evaluate suspension travel and tire deflection.
* Establish a baseline suspension model that can later be used for **suspension parameter optimization**.
* Implement and validate the model using MATLAB/Simulink.

## Vehicle Considered

The vehicle considered for this analysis is the **Tata Harrier**.

The vehicle-level specifications are converted into quarter-car parameters so that the vertical dynamics of one wheel assembly can be represented.

The model uses representative Tata Harrier vehicle parameters and suspension assumptions. Since the complete suspension characteristics of a production vehicle are not publicly available, parameters that are not directly specified by the manufacturer are treated as engineering/modeling assumptions.

This distinction is important because the model is intended for **engineering analysis and comparative optimization**, rather than as an exact manufacturer-supplied suspension model.

## 2-DOF Quarter-Car Representation

The quarter-car model consists of two degrees of freedom:

1. **Sprung mass displacement** — vertical movement of the vehicle body.
2. **Unsprung mass displacement** — vertical movement of the wheel and associated suspension components.

The road profile acts on the tire and generates a vertical disturbance that propagates through the tire and suspension to the vehicle body.

The simplified physical arrangement is:

```text
          Sprung Mass
             mₛ
             │
             │
        ┌────┴────┐
        │ Spring  │ Kₛ
        │ Damper  │ Cₛ
        └────┬────┘
             │
          Unsprung
           Mass
             mᵤ
             │
          Tire
            Kₜ
             │
             │
        Road Profile
             xᵣ
```

## Mathematical Model

The 2-DOF quarter-car system is described using two coupled differential equations.

### Sprung Mass Equation

The forces acting on the sprung mass are generated by the suspension spring and damper.

$$
m_s\ddot{x}_s =
-K_s(x_s-x_u)
-C_s(\dot{x}_s-\dot{x}_u)
$$

### Unsprung Mass Equation

The unsprung mass is affected by the suspension forces and the tire force.

$$
m_u\ddot{x}_u =
K_s(x_s-x_u)
+C_s(\dot{x}_s-\dot{x}_u)
-K_t(x_u-x_r)
$$

where:

* \(x_s\) = sprung mass displacement
* \(x_u\) = unsprung mass displacement
* \(x_r\) = road displacement
* \(m_s\) = sprung mass
* \(m_u\) = unsprung mass
* \(K_s\) = suspension spring stiffness
* \(C_s\) = suspension damping coefficient
* \(K_t\) = tire stiffness

---

## State-Space Representation

The model can also be represented in state-space form.

The selected state variables are:

$$
x_1=x_s
$$

$$
x_2=\dot{x}_s
$$

$$
x_3=x_u
$$

$$
x_4=\dot{x}_u
$$

Therefore, the state vector is:

$$
X=
\begin{bmatrix}
x_s\\
\dot{x}_s\\
x_u\\
\dot{x}_u
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The system can be expressed in the general form:

$$
\dot{X}=AX+BU
$$

$$
Y=CX+DU
$$

where the input is the road displacement and the outputs represent the relevant vehicle and suspension responses.

The state-space representation provides a compact mathematical description of the 2-DOF suspension dynamics and can also be used for future control-system or optimization work.

## MATLAB/Simulink Implementation

The mathematical model was implemented in **MATLAB Simulink**.

The Simulink model uses:

* Sum blocks to calculate the forces acting on each mass.
* Gain blocks to represent mass, spring, damper, and tire parameters.
* Integrators to obtain velocity and displacement from acceleration.
* Feedback connections to represent the coupling between the sprung and unsprung masses.
* A road-profile input to introduce the external disturbance.
* Output blocks to record the resulting suspension responses.

## Road Profile

A controlled road disturbance is used to evaluate the suspension response.

The road profile was modified during the simulation setup so that the primary bump occurs at approximately **1.36 seconds**.

This provides a clearly identifiable disturbance in the simulation output and allows the transient response of the suspension system to be evaluated.

The same road input is used consistently when comparing different suspension configurations so that changes in the output can be attributed to the suspension parameters rather than changes in the road disturbance.

## Performance Metrics

Three primary performance metrics are considered for evaluating the suspension.

### 1. RMS Body Acceleration

RMS body acceleration is used as an indicator of **ride comfort**.

A lower RMS body acceleration generally indicates that less vibration is transmitted to the vehicle body.

$$
a_{RMS}=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T a_s^2(t)\,dt
}
$$

where \(a_s(t)\) is the vertical acceleration of the sprung mass.

---

### 2. RMS Suspension Travel

Suspension travel represents the relative movement between the sprung and unsprung masses.

$$
x_{travel}=x_s-x_u
$$

The RMS suspension travel is calculated as:

$$
x_{travel,RMS}=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T (x_s-x_u)^2\,dt
}
$$

Lower suspension travel generally indicates reduced suspension movement, subject to the trade-off between comfort and suspension stiffness.

---

### 3. RMS Tire Deflection

Tire deflection represents the relative movement between the wheel and the road.

$$
x_{tire}=x_u-x_r
$$

The RMS tire deflection is calculated as:

$$
x_{tire,RMS}=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{T}
\int_0^T (x_u-x_r)^2\,dt
}
$$

This metric provides an indication of the tire's vertical deformation and its ability to follow the road profile.

## Unit Consistency

All displacement quantities in the model are maintained in **meters (m)**, while acceleration is represented in **m/s²**.

For example:

$$
0.0104\;m = 10.4\;mm
$$

Therefore, displacement values that appear small numerically are expected because the model uses SI units.

Maintaining consistent SI units is important when calculating forces, suspension travel, tire deflection, and RMS performance metrics.

## Suspension Optimization

The baseline model provides the starting point for suspension optimization.

The suspension stiffness \(K_s\) and damping coefficient \(C_s\) can be varied to investigate their effect on:

* Ride comfort
* Suspension travel
* Tire deflection

Because improving one performance measure can negatively affect another, suspension tuning is treated as a **multi-objective problem** rather than optimizing a single parameter independently.

The baseline Tata Harrier configuration therefore provides a reference point against which optimized configurations can be compared.

## Limitations

The model is a simplified quarter-car representation of the Tata Harrier and therefore does not represent the complete vehicle suspension system.

The model does not explicitly account for:

* Full vehicle pitch and roll dynamics
* Left-right suspension interaction
* Nonlinear suspension components
* Suspension geometry
* Bushing compliance
* Variable damping
* Detailed tire nonlinearities
* Real-world road irregularities beyond the selected input

The model is therefore intended primarily for **simulation, comparison, and suspension parameter optimization**.
